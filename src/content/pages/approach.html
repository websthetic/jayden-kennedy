Approach

The mechanics, then your decision

Every client gets the same three things: a written analysis, a plain explanation of what it means, and a recommendation I am willing to argue against.

The analysis

The analysis takes an afternoon and I do it by hand, because the automated estimate cannot see the renovation, the lot line or the ninety days of offers that never registered. What you get is a range, the evidence behind each end of it, and the conditions that would move it.

If a property is wrong for you, I will say so. That has cost me commissions and it is the reason people come back.

Read: what a comparative market analysis actually is

Buying

Before we write anything, you will know the carrying cost, the tax bill and what the property is worth if you have to leave in three years. For investment purchases the analysis adds rent evidence, vacancy assumptions written down rather than implied, and debt service coverage at today's rate and at two points higher.

Selling

A listing price is an argument. Mine comes with the comparables attached, so you can see what the number is standing on before you agree to it. The work starts with the last ninety days on your street: what sold, what did not, and what the difference was.
